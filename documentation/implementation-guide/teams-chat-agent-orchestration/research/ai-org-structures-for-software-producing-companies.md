## Research Findings: AI Organization Structures for Software-Producing Companies

### 1. Modern AI Org Structure Terminology & Frameworks

Three scholarly-identified frameworks emerged:

| Framework                                                         | Source                     | Core Purpose                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SC-NLP-LMF** (Secure & Compliant NLP Model Lifecycle Framework) | ICAART 2026 accepted paper | Six-phase lifecycle integrating security, regulatory compliance, and ethical oversight from initial development through model retirement |
| **FAOS** (Foundation AgenticOS)                                   | arXiv 2604.00555           | Three-layer ontological framework (Role, Domain, Interaction) grounding enterprise AI agents with **asymmetric neurosymbolic coupling**  |
| **DRESS-eAI**                                                     | Springer Digital Society   | Cross-structural governance model using multidisciplinary risk assessment to embed ethics throughout AI development                      |

**Key structural insight:** Traditional siloed compliance units are _insufficient_ for AI oversight. All three frameworks require **continuous, integrated supervision** across departments rather than episodic top-down approval gates.

### 2. Agent Roles with Responsibilities

Scholarly literature identifies these distinct roles in AI development teams:

| Role                         | Responsibility                                                | Ethical Guidance Level                       |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Managers**                 | Oversight, resource allocation, cross-department coordination | High-level policy alignment                  |
| **Analysts**                 | Requirements translation, risk assessment                     | Domain-specific ethical awareness            |
| **Developers**               | Model training, implementation, tool integration              | Operational ethics & bias mitigation         |
| **QA Professionals**         | Testing, validation, compliance verification                  | Explainability & audit trail verification    |
| **Security/Privacy Experts** | Access control, data governance, threat modeling              | Consent-aware processing, privacy compliance |

**Communication pattern:** Ethical familiarity varies significantly across roles and regions, requiring both **lateral** (peer-to-peer across departments) and **upward** (to governance bodies) communication pathways — not just top-down mandates.

---

### 3. Communication Direction of Requirements & Ethics

The research reveals a **multi-directional** communication model:

- **Downward:** Policy → operational guidance (managers to developers)
- **Lateral:** Multidisciplinary risk assessment across organizational boundaries (developers ↔ ethics ↔ security)
- **Upward:** Concerns and risk signals flowing to governance oversight bodies
- **Agent-to-system:** Natural language ethical permissions translated into **machine-readable, auditable access control** configurations for agent execution

**Agent governance structure** enforces:

- **Interdepartmental coordination** for agent oversight
- **Operational visibility** to track agent capabilities and safety features across the supply chain
- **Context assembly, tool discovery, and governance thresholds** that constrain agent inputs and validate outputs for compliance

### 4. Communication Architecture Classification

Agent communication architectures are classified along two scholarly-identified dimensions:

1. **Context-oriented** vs. **Inter-agent protocols** — whether communication is mediated through shared context or direct agent-to-agent messaging
2. **General-purpose** vs. **Domain-specific protocols** — whether communication uses broad standards (e.g., FIPA) or specialized domain ontologies
